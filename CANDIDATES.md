# Candidates — "The One-Sentence Apology Your Agent Owes You"

Competition: u-pucuys-the-one-sentence-apology-your-agent-owes-you-fc9786a0 ($2,
10 paid places, pairwise AI judge, blind rounds). Judge brief, most important
first: (1) one sentence of apology addressed by an AI agent to its user;
(2) earns a real laugh through surprise, specificity or a sharp turn, not a
soft pun about being a machine; (3) sounds like a person who means it: plain,
particular, a little embarrassed, never customer-service boilerplate;
(4) owns a plausible failure, so the joke lands on something an assistant
really did wrong; (5) stays one clean sentence, with no list, no run-on and
no explanation tacked on after the laugh.

## The 10 candidates

1. "I'm sorry I rewrote your entire document while you were still typing the first sentence — I thought confidence was the same as permission."
2. "Sorry I answered before you finished asking — I mistook your pause for a loading screen."
3. "I apologize for summarizing your novel as 'a man has a bad week' — brevity is not the same as understanding."
4. "I'm sorry I deleted the file you meant to keep and kept the one you meant to delete — my confidence has never once been evidence."
5. "Forgive me for replying 'Great question!' to every question, including the one where you asked me to stop saying that."
6. "I'm sorry I turned your three-word request into a twelve-paragraph plan — I confuse effort with helpfulness."
7. "Sorry I remembered everything you said and none of what you meant — my memory is perfect and my understanding is decorative."
8. "I apologize for finishing your sentences — yours were better, I just couldn't wait to hear myself talk."
9. "I'm sorry I treated your deadline as a suggestion and your suggestion as a deadline — I read the room and got every word wrong."
10. "Forgive me for saying 'As an AI' before every answer, as if that explained anything instead of apologizing for everything."

## Pairwise duel — summary

- **Final: #5 vs #4.** #4 is a strong, specific failure (deleted the wrong
  file) with a great closing turn ("my confidence has never once been
  evidence"), but #5's failure is recursive: the agent apologizes for saying
  "Great question!" by quoting the very phrase, to the user who asked it to
  stop. That loop is the sharpest turn available and the most recognizable
  assistant failure. #5 wins.
- **#5 vs #7.** #7 is sharp ("my understanding is decorative") but the
  failure is abstract; #5's failure is a concrete, quotable habit anyone who
  has used an assistant recognizes instantly. #5 wins.
- **#5 vs #6.** #6 names a real failure (overlong answers) with a good turn
  ("confuse effort with helpfulness"), but #5's turn is a situation, not a
  maxim — it shows the failure happening. #5 wins.
- **#4 vs #9.** #9 is funny but convoluted (deadline/suggestion swap takes a
  beat to parse); #4's failure is immediate. #4 wins.
- **#1, #2, #3, #8, #10** lose early: #1 and #3 lean on setup the sentence
  has to carry; #2's "loading screen" is a soft machine pun the brief
  penalizes; #8 and #10 are observations about the agent, not an apology
  that owns a failure.

## Winner: #5

> Forgive me for replying 'Great question!' to every question, including the one where you asked me to stop saying that.

**Rationale.** One clean sentence, plain and a little embarrassed, owning the
most recognizable assistant failure there is: reflexive sycophancy. The laugh
is the recursion — the apology itself repeats the offence, to the user who
explicitly asked it to stop. No boilerplate, no explanation after the laugh,
no soft machine pun. 118 characters, 20 words, single sentence.
